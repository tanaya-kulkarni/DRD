Understanding the why

    In the previous section,while we were able to identify dark patterns and some questionable practices, we also thought it was important to understand what led to these decisions in the first place. Hence, we start this section by trying to understand the "why" behind some of the dark patterns.

    Misleading marketing copy

    From “Framing of Information”, we found that UI copies tended to rephrase terms, when they had to be shown directly to users. Why might marketing teams frame a service more favourably than its terms allow?

    • Perhaps technical, restrictive language is seen as a barrier to attracting users.
    • The company may prioritise a clear, appealing message over communicating every limitation upfront.
    • Campaigns may be optimised for sign-ups and conversions, creating pressure to emphasise benefits over conditions.

    Low visibility of terms

    From "Display of Information", we noticed mentions of the privacy policy, terms and conditions made deliberately small and with low contrast.Why might such important terms be displayed in small text or with low contrast?

    • Perhaps making them less prominent is seen as a way to keep the user journey simple and uninterrupted.
    • Designers may struggle to fit extensive legal information into an already crowded screen.
    • Making terms less prominent may help reduce drop-offs during onboarding or transactions.

    Where good intentions can go wrong

    These decisions may begin with seemingly reasonable goals: attracting users or reducing friction. But when those goals take priority over informed choice, they can undermine trust.

    Understanding the pressures a company faces doesn’t justify every decision. It helps us recognise what drives these practices and where change might begin.

    These are our speculations about how these decisions were taken. They are not representative of Phonepe’s actual workings in any way.
